Case Study · 01

Spotify
Redesign

TypeUI/UX Redesign
PlatformMobile App (iOS/Android)
ToolFigma
StatusIn Progress
🎵

01Overview

Spotify is the world's most popular music streaming platform — yet certain design decisions leave users frustrated, confused, or simply unaware of powerful features sitting right in front of them.

This personal redesign project challenges some of those decisions: I've introduced a light mode (which Spotify still doesn't officially offer), rearranged interface elements for faster accessibility, and brought the long-buried Discover feature to the navigation bar where it belongs.

02The Problem

Three core pain points drove this redesign:

🌙 No Light Mode
Spotify's dark-only interface is polarizing. Many users — especially in bright environments — strongly prefer a light alternative. Competing platforms like Apple Music and YouTube Music offer it.
🔍 Hidden Discovery
The Discover feature is buried inside the Search bar. Most users don't even know it exists because of its unconventional placement — it should be a first-class citizen of the app.
⚡ Friction in Core Flows
Common actions like adding to a playlist, sharing a song, or seeing lyrics required too many taps. Elements were placed far from where users naturally thumb-reach on modern phones.
📱 Thumb Zone Ignored
Frequently used controls were placed in hard-to-reach areas of the screen. The redesign moves high-priority actions into the bottom thumb zone for one-handed comfort.

03Design Decisions

"The best design is the one users don't have to think about. Every element should earn its position."

Light Mode: Built a complete light theme system — ensuring all components, player UI, album art overlays, and typography maintained readability and visual harmony. Used warm whites and soft greys to avoid harshness.

Discover on Nav Bar: Moved the Discover feature from the hidden depths of Search to a dedicated nav bar icon. This alone significantly improves feature visibility without adding cognitive load to the UI.

Restructured Player Screen: Reorganized the now-playing screen to surface actions users most frequently perform — like adding to library, lyrics toggle, and song queue — at fingertip reach.

Navigation Simplification: Collapsed some of the redundant navigation paths and consolidated related sections (e.g., Podcasts + Audiobooks combined into a "Listen" tab).

Light Mode System Discover on Nav Thumb Zone Optimization Component Redesign Typography System Color System Interaction States

04Key Takeaways

This redesign reinforced that even mature, well-funded products leave room for meaningful UX improvements. Small changes — like where you put a button — can dramatically change how many users discover a feature.

The light mode work also taught me a lot about building a proper dual-theme design system in Figma, using variables and component states efficiently.

Food Delivery
Website

PlatformWeb (Responsive)
ToolFigma
StatusIn Progress
🍔

01Overview

Platforms like Zomato and Swiggy dominate food delivery in India — but they're solving the same problem in the same way. This project explores what a next-generation food delivery platform could look like if we designed around community, transparency, and discovery rather than just speed and discounts.

The platform is tentatively called Thaali — a web-first food experience that's social, transparent, and deeply local.

02What Makes It Different

🔴 Live Kitchen Cam
Restaurants can opt into showing a live feed of their kitchen. Users can actually see their food being prepared — building trust in a way no review can match.
👥 Group Ordering Mode
Friends can create a shared cart — each person adds their own items, sees what others chose, and splits payment automatically. Perfect for office lunches and group dining.
🏘️ Hyperlocal Discovery
A "Your Lane" section surfaces hidden local gems — home kitchens, small tiffin services, and neighbourhood dabbawalas — that never rank on Zomato's algorithm.
🌱 Sustainability Score
Every restaurant gets a sustainability rating based on packaging, food waste practices, and sourcing. Users who prefer eco-conscious eating can filter by this score.
💬 Dish Reviews (Not Restaurant)
Reviews are tied to individual dishes, not just the restaurant overall. You can see exactly how many people liked a specific item before ordering it.
📅 Meal Planning
Users can schedule weekly meal plans and get automatic reminders. Regular orders from favourite restaurants are remembered and suggested each week.

03Design Approach

"The goal wasn't to make another food app. It was to make one that felt like a trusted friend who knows your city's food scene."

Homepage: A warm, editorial-style landing page — less grid of restaurants, more food storytelling. Large hero imagery, curated collections ("Best Thalis Near You", "Hidden Gems"), and contextual time-based suggestions (breakfast vs dinner).

Restaurant Page: Redesigned around dishes, not descriptions. Each dish has its own mini-card with ratings, ingredients, customization options, and a "most ordered" badge.

Checkout Flow: Stripped down to 3 steps: Review Cart → Delivery + Payment → Confirm. Group mode shows a live summary of who's ordering what.

Design System: Warm amber and deep terracotta colour palette. Syne for headings, DM Sans for body. Generous white space with food photography as the hero visual element.

04Current Progress

Currently in the wireframing and low-fidelity prototyping phase. The homepage, restaurant listing, and single restaurant pages are designed. Checkout and user profile are in progress.

Case Study · 03

Smart
Sanstha

TypeBE Major Project · EdTech
My RoleUI/UX Designer
Team4 Members
ToolFigma
⚖️

01Overview

SmartSanstha is a comprehensive digital platform designed to spread awareness about the Indian Constitution and make it genuinely accessible to every citizen — regardless of age, educational background, or prior legal knowledge.

The Indian Constitution is the foundation of democracy, yet most citizens have never read even a single article of it. Traditional methods of learning — textbooks, lectures, government PDFs — are dry, complex, and deeply unappealing, especially for younger generations.

SmartSanstha solves this with a gamified learning approach where users discover constitutional articles through interactive games, simulations, and adaptive challenges.

02The Problem

"448 articles. 12 schedules. 25 parts. Most Indians have read zero of them — not because they don't care, but because no one made it interesting."

Research revealed that the primary barriers to constitutional literacy in India are:

📖 Complexity of Language
Constitutional text is written in dense legal language that's hard to parse even for educated adults, let alone students or rural citizens.
😴 Boring Traditional Methods
Textbooks and lecture formats create passive learning — no engagement, no retention, and no motivation to continue beyond exams.
🌐 Accessibility Gaps
Existing resources are largely in English, lack visual aids, and aren't designed for mobile-first or low-literacy users.
❓ No Practical Context
People don't know how constitutional articles affect their daily lives — the content feels abstract and disconnected from real-world situations.

03Core Features

SmartSanstha combines five key modules into one cohesive platform:

🎮 Constitutional Games
Users learn articles through mini-games — scenario-based puzzles, memory matching, constitutional trivia challenges, and story-driven missions that embed legal knowledge naturally.
🏛️ Courtroom Simulation
An immersive courtroom experience where users argue real constitutional cases as either the prosecution or defense. AI generates opposing arguments based on actual articles, making it educational and thrilling.
🤖 AI Constitution Chatbot
A conversational AI assistant trained on the Indian Constitution. Users can ask questions in plain language — "What are my rights if I'm arrested?" — and get accurate, article-backed answers.
📊 Adaptive Quizzes
The quiz engine adapts to each user's knowledge level. Beginners get simpler scenario questions; advanced users face complex multi-article reasoning challenges. Progress is tracked across sessions.
👤 User Dashboard
A personal progress hub — showing articles learned, quiz scores, game achievements, streaks, and a learning roadmap. Gamification elements (badges, leaderboards, XP) drive consistent engagement.
📚 Article Library
A simplified, beautifully designed library of all 448 articles — presented in plain language with real-world examples, related articles, and links to landmark court cases.

04My Role — UI/UX Design

"My responsibility was to take a complex, dense subject and design an interface that made it feel approachable, even exciting."

Information Architecture: Mapped out the entire platform structure — 6 modules, user onboarding, progression system, and cross-linking between articles, games, and quiz results. Built a complete IA diagram and user flow in Figma.

Onboarding Flow: Designed a gentle onboarding experience where new users select their interest level, learning goals, and preferred language. This personalizes their homepage and game recommendations from day one.

Gamification Design: Created the visual language for XP points, achievement badges, streak counters, and leaderboards. Every game screen was designed to feel visually engaging without distracting from the educational content.

Courtroom Simulation UI: Designed one of the most complex screens — a courtroom interface with a split debate view, article reference panel, timer, and AI response area. Balanced information density with clarity.

Accessibility: Ensured all screens met WCAG contrast requirements. Designed for multiple font sizes and built a layout system that works for both English and Devanagari script.

Design System: Built a comprehensive component library in Figma — buttons, cards, quiz blocks, game boards, navigation patterns, iconography, and a full colour system using teal and deep navy as primary palette.
