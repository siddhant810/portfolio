Flex Academy — one page, two audiences

A landing page and booking system for a short-term-rental education brand — from the founders of The Flex and Base360. One page had to speak to total beginners and seasoned operators, then route each into the right conversion flow. Designed end-to-end in Figma: UX, UI, interactive prototype, and a reusable component library.

01Overview

Flex Academy is a concept brand that teaches people how to build a short-term-rental business — spun up by the teams behind The Flex and Base360.

The brief was a single marketing page that had to do two jobs at once: welcome a complete beginner thinking about their first unit, and speak credibly to an operator who already runs a few. I designed the whole thing in Figma — research, wireframes, final UI, an interactive prototype, and the component library behind it.

02The problem

How do you make one page feel hand-made for two people who are nothing alike?

A beginner and an operator arrive with opposite fears. Talk only to beginners and you lose credibility with pros; talk only to pros and you scare off newcomers. The page needed a clear fork early — without splitting into two separate sites.

The beginner

"Where do I even start?"

Thinking about their first unit. Needs reassurance, a low-commitment first step, and proof it's learnable.

The operator

"Can you help me scale?"

Already runs a few units. Wants systems, strategy, and software — not a beginner's webinar.

03The idea — two paths

The whole page pivots on one decision point: "Where are you starting from?" Pick a lane and the copy, CTA, and conversion flow all change with you. Launch is the free, friendly on-ramp; Scale is the high-intent, operator track.

04Brand direction

The palette borrows from both parents. Cream and green come from The Flex — warm, calm, hospitality-led — and carry the whole page. Indigo comes from Base360 and is treated as a system colour: it only ever appears on the Scale path and anything software-related, so the fork is reinforced by colour, not just words.

Cream#F6F1E6 · canvas
Flex Green#1C6B4F · primary
Deep Green#0F4E39 · ink
Base360 Indigo#4338CA · Scale only

Rule: green leads everywhere; indigo is reserved. The moment a visitor sees indigo, they're on the operator / software track — a quiet signal that keeps a two-audience page from feeling like two brands.

11Honest notes

This is a concept project. Flex Academy isn't a live product — I built it to practise designing for two audiences on one page and to prototype real conversion flows end-to-end in Figma.

Photography is stock, the Base360 dashboard is an AI-generated mock, and prices, dates, and founder details are placeholders. The UX decisions, flows, brand logic, and component system are mine.

12Takeaways

One page can serve two people

A single clear fork beats two separate pages — if the copy, colour, and CTA all commit to the choice.

Colour can carry logic

Reserving indigo for Scale turned a brand colour into a wayfinding tool with zero extra UI.

Design the error, not just the happy path

Empty, error, and guardrail states (like the zero-units reroute) are where a prototype earns trust.

Components make copy cheap

Variants + auto-layout meant I could iterate wording and sections fast without rebuilding screens.
